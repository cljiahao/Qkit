# qkit Go-To-Market Handbook

**Date:** 2026-06-22
**For:** solo founder · ~10 hrs/week · ~$0 budget · validate-first
**Basis:** deep research (23 sources, 21 verified claims, 4 refuted). Verified
facts are cited; pilot scripts / channel rankings / gates / roadmap are reasoned
synthesis on top of that evidence (labelled where so).

> **Blunt headline:** the evidence contradicts the earlier assumption that
> pasar malam / Ramadan bazaars are the wedge. For a $0, 10-hr/week solo founder,
> the highest-leverage first segment is **recurring weekend artisan/food
> markets** (Makers' Market, by Invade). Don't start at the bazaars.

---

## 1. Beachhead — recurring weekend markets (NOT bazaars)

**Pick: recurring weekend artisan/food markets** (Makers' Market / Invade —
PLQ, i12 Katong, Dhoby Ghaut; weekend + weekday cadences). [makersmarket.sg]

Why it wins for _your_ constraints:

- **One organiser = many vendors.** Invade concentrates a recurring vendor pool
  behind a single relationship (`spaces@invade.co`, one mailing list, per-venue
  booking portals, IG/FB). A 10-hr/week solo founder can reach dozens of repeat
  vendors through one intro. [makersmarket.sg]
- **Cadence matches the product.** Single-weekend events fit the per-event-day
  pass exactly, and a weekly cadence produces **retention signal in days, not a
  year** — critical for validate-first.
- **Low vendor sunk cost** → higher willingness to try cheap software.

Why **not** the alternatives (evidence):

- **Pasar malam:** runs **16 days–1 month with full-period commitment** —
  vendors are locked into continuous multi-day trading, not the single-day
  cadence your pass fits. Vendor FB groups / Carousell exist but are
  discovery/gatekept channels, risky for cold product push. [tlkevents.com,
  kitchenunionsg.com]
- **Ramadan bazaars (Geylang Serai):** the **worst** first segment despite huge
  concentration. Full-period rental **S$8k–15k** (capped S$15k since 2024,
  excl. fixtures), all-in **~S$19k–30k+**, and many vendors **don't target
  break-even** — they're there for brand awareness. A per-day app pass is
  structurally mispriced here, and it's a **once-a-year** window (2026: 14 Feb–
  21 Mar). [dollarsandsense.sg, mothership.sg ×2]

**Risk that can kill this beachhead (must de-risk first):** do Invade / market
organisers **allow a third-party ordering tool** at their markets, or do they
have POS/exclusivity arrangements? One organiser "no" closes the lane. → Your
_first_ conversation is with an organiser, not a vendor (see §2).

## 2. Validation & first pilot (synthesis, evidence-anchored)

Goal: **1–3 pilot vendors at one real market weekend**, $0, inside ~3–4 weeks.

**Step 1 — Get organiser permission (the gating move).**
Email `spaces@invade.co` (and DM the Makers' Market IG). Pitch qkit as a _vendor
perk that makes their market look modern_, not a vendor you're selling to. Ask:
"Can I run a free pilot with 1–2 F&B stalls at an upcoming weekend?" Offer them
the aggregate data (orders, peak times) as the hook. If they say yes, the vendor
intro is warm and the exclusivity risk is dead.

**Step 2 — Recruit 1–3 F&B vendors** (markets are ~¼ F&B of 60+ stalls).
Pitch in vendor language: "Free this weekend. Customers scan, order, and see when
it's ready — you stop shouting numbers and the queue moves faster. No app for
anyone, no payment changes, you keep your own cash/PayNow." Set it up _for_ them
on the day (you're on-site anyway).

**Step 3 — Run it, measure.** A pilot is a **success** if, at one booth on one
day:

- ≥ 50% of customers who scan complete an order (scan→order conversion),
- the vendor says the queue/ordering felt smoother (qualitative), AND
- **the vendor asks to use it again next event** (the only retention signal that
  matters — see §6).

What to capture: scans, orders placed, scan→order %, orders/hour at peak, and a
one-line vendor verbal. You already have the funnel + events table to log most of
this.

## 3. Pricing & packaging

**Per-day rent frame** (what "cheap" means to these vendors): ~**$40–500/day**;
TLK F&B **$180–350/day** ($200–300 typical); Invade **$48–500/day**.
[tlkevents.com, dollarsandsense.sg] A software day-pass must read as a rounding
error against that — think **low-single-digit to ~$10–15/day**, not %-of-sales.
(A refuted "$500–several-thousand/day" figure — do **not** anchor on it.)

**Competitor monetisation is different from yours** (so compete on UX, not price
parity):

- **Rewardly** GROWTH from **S$39/mo** (S$59 non-NEA), incl. QR ordering +
  payments; plus a **FLEXI "From SGD0"** event plan monetised via hardware + card
  MDR. [rewardly.sg/pricing]
- **HitPay**: pure **pay-per-transaction**, no monthly/setup/platform fee,
  hardware from ~S$85. [hitpayapp.com/sg/pricing]
- Both **bundle payment processing**; qkit deliberately does **not**. Your wedge
  is **app-free ordering + live queue UX**, priced to pay only when trading —
  cheaper than a monthly sub for sporadic traders.

**Free tier + the "pay-once-keep-many-booths" leak:** already solved in-product
— free = **1 live booth, swappable, others paused** (booth-serveability,
migration 0016). Keep that. The day-pass unlocks the live booth for an event day;
Pro (monthly) suits vendors trading most weekends. Decide the actual numbers by
**testing** the pass price against the $40–500/day band during the pilot — it's
an open question, not a verified figure.

## 4. Acquisition engine (organic, ranked by effort-vs-return)

1. **Organiser partnerships (highest ROI).** One Invade/market-organiser
   relationship = recurring access to a refreshing vendor pool + implicit
   endorsement. Offer aggregate market data / a co-branded "order here" sign.
   Repeatable across each Invade venue and then other market operators. _Lowest
   effort, highest return — make this the spine._
2. **On-the-ground word-of-mouth at the market.** You're on-site for pilots;
   adjacent stalls watch a smoother queue and ask. Vendor-to-vendor referral
   inside one market is the natural loop. _Medium effort, compounding._
3. **Vendor FB groups / Carousell (lowest priority).** Real $0 channels but
   gatekept and discovery-oriented — easy to read as spam. Use only to _listen_
   and warm-intro, not to broadcast. [kitchenunionsg.com]

Skip content/SEO for now — too slow for a 10-hr/week validate-first phase.

## 5. Regulatory / ops reality check (SG)

- **Vendor food licensing is NOT your burden.** Temporary-fair food vendors get
  a **free Temporary Fair Stall Licence**, processed by the fair operator under a
  flat **$60 fair permit** — no separate vendor fee, valid only during the fair.
  You can truthfully tell vendors qkit doesn't change their licensing.
  [gobusiness.gov.sg ×2]
- **Payments out of scope = no MAS exposure.** Because qkit never touches money,
  you avoid payment-services licensing entirely. Keep it that way early; adding
  payments later is a _deliberate, heavy_ decision.
- **GST:** compulsory registration only above **S$1M** taxable turnover
  (retrospective/prospective) — a non-issue at pilot/early scale. [IRAS]
- **PDPA:** _applies_ to the customer data you collect (names, order history).
  The specific claim that PDPA forces you to appoint **and publicise** a DPO was
  **refuted** in research — treat as **open**; confirm the exact obligation
  against PDPC primary sources before writing any compliance statement. Minimum
  sane posture now: collect the least data, don't sell it, have a short privacy
  note.

## 6. Metrics & decision gates

**Funnel (per pilot):** scans → orders (activation) → vendor-reuse (retention).

**Retention method (important):** use **rolling retention anchored to each
vendor's first event** and matched to **event cadence** — measure "did the vendor
come back for their _next event_", not "next calendar week." Vendors churn
_between_ events by design; calendar cohorts would distort it. [Mixpanel docs]

**Go / no-go gates for the validate→decide fork:**

- **Gate A (after 1–3 pilots):** organiser allows it **and** ≥1 vendor asks to
  reuse next event → continue. If no vendor reuses → the wedge is unproven; fix
  the product or rethink the segment before spending more time.
- **Gate B (after ~5–10 vendor-events):** **vendor next-event reuse ≥ ~50%** and
  at least one vendor willing to **pay** a day-pass → you have a real wedge →
  _then_ choose lifestyle vs venture. Below that → stay in validation, don't
  scale.

(Gate thresholds are my recommendation, not a verified benchmark — calibrate as
real numbers arrive.)

## 7. Staged roadmap

- **0–3 months — Prove the wedge.** _One goal:_ get a **yes from an organiser**
  and run **1–3 pilot vendor-events**, hitting Gate A. Engineering: clear the P1
  launch-readiness items (done) + enable Supabase backups. Nothing speculative.
- **3–6 months — Repeatability.** _One goal:_ convert pilots into **paying
  day-pass vendors across ≥2 market venues** and hit Gate B (≥~50% next-event
  reuse). Tighten pricing to the tested number; lean on the organiser channel.
- **6–12 months — Decide & deepen.** _One goal:_ based on Gate-B data, **commit
  to lifestyle or venture** and double down on the single best channel. Only now
  consider a second segment (pasar malam's longer commitment, or caterers'
  higher ticket) or the sibling -kit API.

## What's unproven / refuted (read before acting)

- **qkit's own price isn't set** — validate the day-pass against $40–500/day rent
  and $39–59/mo competitor subs during the pilot.
- **Organiser openness is assumed, not verified** — the whole beachhead hinges on
  it; test it first.
- **Refuted claims — do not rely on:** "$500–several-thousand/day pasar malam
  rent"; "30+ Ramadan bazaar locations in 2026"; the explicit-break-even-goal
  framing; the PDPA appoint-and-publicise-DPO obligation.
- Pricing/event figures are 2025–26 dated — re-check live pages before quoting.
