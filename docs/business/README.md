# business

## Purpose

Go-to-market and business-strategy documentation.

## Contents

- `2026-06-22-gtm-handbook.md` — the qkit GTM handbook (dated 2026-06-22), written for a solo founder at ~10 hrs/week and ~$0 budget, based on a 23-source deep-research pass (21 verified claims, 4 refuted). Concludes the beachhead should be **recurring weekend artisan/food markets** (e.g. Makers' Market / Invade), not pasar malam or Ramadan bazaars as originally assumed — those have longer full-period rental commitments that don't fit a per-event-day pass. Covers: the validation plan (get organiser permission first, then recruit 1–3 F&B vendors, measure scan→order conversion + vendor reuse), pricing guidance (day-pass in the low-single-digit to ~$10–15/day range, competing on UX rather than matching subscription competitors like Rewardly/HitPay), the organic acquisition ranking (organiser partnerships > on-the-ground word-of-mouth > vendor Facebook groups), Singapore regulatory notes (vendor food licensing isn't qkit's burden, no MAS exposure since qkit never touches payments, GST/PDPA thresholds), go/no-go metric gates (Gate A: ≥1 vendor reuse after 1–3 pilots; Gate B: ≥~50% next-event reuse after 5–10 vendor-events plus a paying vendor), and a staged 0–3/3–6/6–12 month roadmap. Ends with an explicit "what's unproven / refuted" section flagging qkit's own pricing as untested and listing the four refuted research claims so they aren't relied on later.
